## 10. Repeated receptions: an error rate that needs no reconstruction

Pairs of census entries under the SAME key whose plaintexts have the SAME length and agree position by position on at least a fraction f of positions (an unknown character counts as agreeing) are taken to be two copies of one cryptogram (a second intercept or a repeat). The true ciphertext of both is then the same string, so the two LOG texts can be compared directly, with no decryption and no repair: every disagreement is an error in at least one copy. Alignment: unit costs, '-' free on either side. Per-copy rate = disagreements / (2 x symbols), which assumes the two copies' errors are independent and never coincide (so it is if anything an underestimate).

This matters because in class A the plaintext was obtained by decrypting the log text itself, so a wrong symbol (E5) re-encrypts to itself and is invisible to sections 2-4.
